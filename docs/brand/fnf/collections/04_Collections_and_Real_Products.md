## Collection strategy

Collections should help customers shop, not make a rigid taxonomy of every possible hobby.

### Everyday Essentials — likely lead category

**Concept:** The daily uniform of free time.  
**Best initial product candidates:** Actual F&FT hats, F&FT logo shirt, hourglass shirt, tumbler if available.  
**Tone:** No drama; good hat, good tee, recognizable mark.  
**Visual:** Neutral-background product photos and natural garage/coffee lifestyle.

### High Octane — already used on site

**Concept:** Garage culture, cars, bikes, builds and custom mechanical aesthetics.  
**Products:** Approved vehicle graphic tees, real embroidered logo gear, accessories.  
**Story:** Tie garments to real FNF projects rather than generic stock racing imagery.

### Masters — already used on site

**Concept:** Experience, craftsmanship and the people who have stories worth hearing.  
**Avoid:** Describing this as an official Masters824 collaboration unless agreed in writing. The collection name can remain independent, with individual maker features or future collaborative capsules.

### Flightline — proposed

**Concept:** Aviation heritage, open sky, travel, aircraft art and expressive prints.  
**Concept board:** Smoke Show, Wingman, Low Pass Legend.  
**Launch status:** Preview editorial / coming-soon inspiration only *if clearly labeled and approved*; do not sell without original artwork, licensed imagery, production proofs and inventory.

### Limited Drops — merchandising attribute, not necessarily a permanent shop category

Limited means physically limited: record edition size, remaining quantity and whether a restock is possible. Never make artificial scarcity claims.

## Suggested first merchandising sequence

1. **Lead with physical stock:** At least one tee and one hat, shown faithfully with size, fit, material and availability.
2. **Bundle related styles editorially:** The hourglass shirt tells the brand story; F&FT cap is a recognizable daily staple.
3. **Develop truthful product details:** Front/back image, print close-up, actual fit/care, size chart and shipping/returns.
4. **Launch a behind-the-scenes story:** "The first batch" with actual photos of hats and manufacturing details if verified.
5. **Build a concept gallery:** Let visitors see what's being developed without confusing concepts with stocked merch.

## Product page template

- Accurate product title + 1-2 sentence specific introduction
- Price/currency, variant selector, stock states and image thumbnails
- Product-true primary photo; secondary front, back, detail, scale/lifestyle views
- Measured size chart + actual blanks/fit
- Composition, printing/decoration method, fabric care, manufacturing source where known
- Shipping/returns/fulfillment details and contact information
- Relevant product/story/collection links, not cross-sells from imaginary catalogs
- Accessibility: meaningful image alt text, readable controls, keyboard support

## Concept record → product lifecycle

```text
Idea / reference
  → Creative concept (photos, prompts, source rights, comments)
  → Approved art direction
  → Original print artwork acquired/recreated with rights cleared
  → Vendor production proof + costing
  → Sample accepted
  → Sellable product: SKUs, variants, price, stock, fulfillment
  → Published and merchandised in collection
```

## CMS essentials (portable beyond FNF)

Do not build new separate editor systems just for these items. Model `Brand`, `Design Concept`, `Artwork Asset`, `Product`, `Collection`, `Campaign`, `Editorial Story/Project`, `Creator/Collaborator`, `Media`, `Approval/Version` as related but distinct resources. Brand rules and visual page sections can be reusable across templates. The source product record owns commerce truth, *not* a duplicate page block.

## Product photography instructions

- **Catalog:** Neutral lighting, exact real garment details, no invented logos/fabric textures, consistent ratios and honest scale.
- **Editorial:** Styled setting is allowed; preserve true product and avoid passing AI-altered logo details as product truth.
- **Close-up:** Print, stitch, patch and fit proof; sometimes the original sharp crop is better than a regenerated photo.
- **Video:** A stationary turntable or slow camera move around real item; no need for risky motion scenes.
- **QC:** Compare reference and final side by side. Reject swapped spelling, badge shape, colorway, pocket or inaccurate art.
