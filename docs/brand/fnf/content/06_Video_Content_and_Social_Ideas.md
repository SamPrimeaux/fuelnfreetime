# 06 — Video, Photo & Social Content Playbook

**Audience:** Justin with limited free time and a phone; Sam editing, organizing, posting after approval.  
**Status:** Idea bank, not scripted dialogue.  
**Related:** `07_Dirt_Bike_Project_and_Masters824.md`, `14_Prompt_Library_for_Justin.md`

## Guiding principle: document, don't perform a fake commercial

Justin doesn't need to be a scripted influencer. Short authentic clips—what he is working on, what he is excited about, who he is meeting, why a shirt design matters—are stronger building blocks than generic cinematic filler. A single real afternoon can support a short Reel, several product images, an Instagram carousel, and a paragraph for the website.

Avoid filming while driving, riding or operating equipment. Capture stationary setups, parked vehicles, trained-professional demonstrations with appropriate permissions and safety management if any are part of a separately organized event. Don't stage risky stunts for content.

## Repeatable video series

### Series A: **The Good Hours**

Episodes about what people choose to make time for: a friend rebuilding a car, someone packing for a lake weekend, a retiree's favorite project, Justin at a local shop, a quiet early morning getting ready.

- Hook: "This is what the weekend is for."
- Story beats: who / what they love / why it matters / what they're doing next.
- Format: 25–60 second profile, candid voice over detail shots, plus optional longer interview.
- Follow-up: link to an approved story/collection, not a forced sales pitch.

### Series B: **Behind the Badge**

Follow real F&FT products from first sketch or logo proof to embroidered patch, printed shirt, packaging or sample check.

- Clip options: "Our first caps," "What actually came back from the shop," "The detail we fixed," "Gold logo: concept vs physical sample."
- Best value: honesty and craftsmanship make simple products meaningful.
- Never claim one material or process if it is only a rendering.

### Series C: **Garage Talk**

A relaxed recurring conversation: what are you working on, what's the weirdest project that took all weekend, what project would you revisit, what was your first ride or car memory, which FNF design would you actually wear?

- Camera can stay on bench/hat/product while someone talks off camera.
- Avoid pressuring anyone to be on screen; get permission.
- Keep it accessible, light and short enough to capture casually.

### Series D: **Projects, Not Promises**

A progressing documentary project, e.g. the dirt bike: first look, inspiration, wrap proof, production visit, final parked beauty shots and what was learned. This can exist independently from any future promotion.

### Series E: **Find Your Fuel**

Celebrates diverse hobbies: coffee rituals, boats at dock, model making, photography, detailing a parked car, aviation museum visit, restoration and family outings. The product is incidental; the passion is the hero.

## Practical shot checklist — one session

**Phone prep:** Clean lens, enough charge and storage; use the highest practical camera setting; shoot some 16:9 landscape and 9:16 vertical takes; get consent; avoid visible private information and unauthorized music. 

**On location:**

1. Wide establishing shot (where are we? stationary camera).
2. Medium shot of Justin arriving or explaining the situation.
3. Three to five safe detail shots of the object/setting.
4. Ten seconds of natural ambient sound.
5. One unscripted question: "What makes this worth your time?"
6. Honest moment from the interaction (with permission).
7. Closing reflection and what's next.
8. Photo of the people together and a few clean thumbnail stills.
9. Product insert if he is actually wearing FNF gear.
10. Record correct names/credits and whether media is approved for brand reuse.

**Post session:** Back up originals; identify the best 10 clips; note date/place/people; upload to the shared source folder; give Sam a voice note saying what happened and which pieces are approved.

## Short video structure, not a memorized script

- **0–3 sec:** Show something real (shirt patch, the bike, logo file, a person arriving) and a simple question/statement.
- **3–20 sec:** Three truthful beats—what's happening, one detail, what makes it interesting.
- **20–35 sec:** Resolution or next installment.
- **End:** Light invitation: "More on the site," "What would you put on this?" or "Next chapter soon." Avoid mandatory engagement tricks.

## Formats from one shoot

- **Reel/Short** (vertical, 15–60 sec): one clear idea.
- **Carousel** (4–8 stills): before, after, details, honest short captions.
- **Editorial page** (wide photos + 150–450 words): the true narrative, credits and product links if relevant.
- **Email** (one good image + useful update): a brief progress note, not a hard sell every week.
- **Product video** (5–15 seconds): stationary garment or cap turntable/detail movement.

## A busy-person publishing rhythm

**Justin:** Capture one *real thing* when it happens; leave a 30–90 second voice note to contextualize.  
**Sam:** Organize, draft two captions, make one crop for each channel, flag anything factually questionable.  
**Justin:** Approve one preferred caption/thumbnail asynchronously.  
**Publish:** Only approved claims and media; archive originals and approvals.

This is better than promising seven videos a week, then inventing content to fill a calendar.

## Thumbnail / composition rules

- Product/craft feature: one strong object, minimal typography, high contrast.
- Keep any logo or print detail legible at phone size.
- Avoid fake creator quotes, pretend partnerships or made-up action shots.
- For actual shirt photos: keep an unaltered reference image available alongside edited creative.

## Questions Justin can answer on voice

"What happened?" "What surprised you?" "Who should be credited?" "What's the next thing?" "Which of these 5 clips would *you* watch?"  
This is enough for Sam to create a strong outline without demanding an on-camera performance.
