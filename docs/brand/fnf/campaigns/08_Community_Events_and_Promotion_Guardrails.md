# 08 — Community, Small Activations & Prize-Promotion Guardrails

**Status:** Proposed activities. Nothing here is scheduled or live.  
**Related:** `07_Dirt_Bike_Project_and_Masters824.md`, `10_Launch_Roadmap_and_Responsibilities.md`

## Build a real community before claiming one

Community is not a counter widget, invented testimonial, or a newsletter-list total. It becomes meaningful when real people contribute stories, share experiences, trust the brand and have a reason to return.

**Suggested FNF community behaviors:**

- Invite people to tell the story behind their real projects and weekends.
- Publish opt-in customer/enthusiast features with creator credit and photo permission.
- Organize low-pressure coffee/garage conversations in authorized accessible spaces.
- Collaborate with local small businesses on product photography and making.
- Highlight experienced makers teaching younger enthusiasts about repair, care and craft in a supervised, appropriate setting.
- Document real attendance, volunteer help and authentic outcomes when events actually happen.

## Low-complexity activation ideas

### 1. "What Fuels Your Free Time?" submissions

Invite customers to submit a short story, safe photo of an object/project and a sentence about why it matters. Obtain explicit publication permission and avoid showing private addresses/location data. Feature one story at a time; avoid fabricated public submission volume.

### 2. The Saturday Coffee Table

A small informal gathering at a venue with permission, where people can show photos of their projects, meet FNF and inspect actual apparel. Plan accessibility, capacity, responsible hosts and any needed venue approval. No risky vehicle demonstrations required.

### 3. Maker Feature Series

Interview a mechanic, wrap designer, screen printer, coffee roaster, builder or retiree about what they enjoy making. Give credit, share their own channels with approval, and keep product advertising separate from organic praise.

### 4. A public art-direction vote

Offer two approved graphic concepts and ask for aesthetic reactions. Clearly distinguish this from a competition or a promised production drop. "Which design should we work on next?" is a legitimate feedback question even if neither is made.

### 5. Small thank-you items

A genuine, plainly described branded sticker or postcard in fulfilled orders can make a purchase memorable. Document real stock and make sure the item isn't framed as buying an opportunity for a prize.

### 6. A simple website story archive

Content areas: `People`, `Projects`, `Merch Making`, `Good Hours`. Start small. A real story with four photos beats a fake event calendar.

## About the high-value dirt-bike giveaway idea

A high-value prize campaign is **not** just another Instagram growth tactic. It brings legal, operational, financial, tax, eligibility, publicity-rights and consumer-protection responsibilities. In particular, making purchase a required route to a random prize is not an acceptable default. Merch purchases should **not** be represented as purchasing a chance to win.

**Until qualified adult business operators obtain appropriate legal review:**

- Do not publish a promised prize, winner, entry mechanism, odds, timeline or eligibility rules.
- Do not build an entry checkout, tickets, payment-linked chances or "buy X gear for entries" workflow.
- Do not claim the promotion is sponsored by Masters824 or another creator without a signed agreement.
- Treat the bike documentary, website launch and real merchandise as independent activities with value on their own.
- Consult a qualified promotions attorney and appropriate business/insurance/tax professionals **before** any promotional mechanic, public announcement or rules are set.

This kit intentionally **does not** supply prize-entry or sales-to-chances tactics.

## Safer, higher-value immediate priorities

- Improve official storefront legitimacy and on-time fulfillment.
- Publish the actual bike story with permissions.
- Improve product trust through verified photos, sizing, shipping and support.
- Build an opt-in email list with clear expectations.
- Develop meaningful collaborations and events without making large prize promises.

## Community page content structure

1. Hero: *"Different rides. Same reason we show up."*
2. One genuine founder-led introduction.
3. Current **real** people/project stories (if permission exists).
4. A way to submit a story or subscribe for updates.
5. Only confirmed upcoming gatherings, with real venue/date/organizer information.
6. Credit and consent policy for submitted images.
7. Honest empty states: "We're putting our first stories together" is stronger than fictional stats.

## Publish checklist

- [ ] Activity is actually taking place.
- [ ] Venue/hosts/participants have consented appropriately.
- [ ] Safety, accessibility, minors' privacy and transport considerations reviewed by responsible adults.
- [ ] Any partnership, donation, contest or promotion terms reviewed as needed.
- [ ] Claims, dates and photos fact-checked.
- [ ] One person owns any contact request/follow-up.
