## Target voice

FNF should sound like a real friend with a real hobby who can tell a story over coffee or leaning on a toolbox. Not a venture-capital pitch, corporate lifestyle agency, caricatured biker or inspirational poster.

| More of this | Less of this |
|---|---|
| "We finally got a few of these made." | "Our disruptive apparel experience has arrived." |
| "It's the stuff you can't stop talking about." | "A movement redefining the culture of modern freedom." |
| Honest jokes, concrete details, short lines | Manufactured swagger and wall-to-wall profanity |
| Pride in small batches and real people | Claiming millions of fans or legendary history |
| Good photos doing half the talking | Twenty buzzwords stacked over a stock image |
| "We're still figuring out the next run." | False scarcity or nonexistent product promises |

## Brand-language ladder

**Brand belief:** The best hours are yours to live.  
**Primary headline candidate:** THE BEST HOURS AREN'T ON THE CLOCK.  
**Secondary brand line:** Fuel what moves you.  
**Already-on-product line:** Earn Your Freedom.  
**Energetic campaign line:** Clock out. Fire up.  
**Community invitation:** Different rides. Same reason we show up.

Pick one primary brand line; don't display all of them at once in a header or on one product page.

### Email ideas

**Story:** "What Justin picked up this weekend." / A link to an authentic story instead of an immediate sales push.

## How edgy is right?

**Level 1 — Friendly:** Storefront support, product details, About. Simple, warm and trustworthy.  
**Level 2 — Garage:** Product captions and organic content. Light sarcasm, small inside jokes, irreverence.  
**Level 3 — Explicit:** Optional artist-approved graphics for a specific audience. Never default brand copy, never an excuse to demean customers or misrepresent affiliations.

An existing custom-car concept contains profanity. Keep it as an **unapproved design idea**, not automatically a public-facing FNF collection piece. Ask Justin whether that's in his voice and what boundaries he wants for products, advertising and community spaces.

## Language to keep out until verified

- "Handmade in Louisiana" / "cut and sewn here" unless manufacturing evidence supports it.
- "Veteran owned," "official collaboration," "limited to 100," or "sold out" unless each is true.
- Invented founding timestamps, real-life conversations or dialogue.
- Made-up member counts, testimonials, events and partnerships.
- Fake product quality promises (performance, durability, material composition).
