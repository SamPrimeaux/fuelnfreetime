# Fuel & Free Time Business and Brand Dossier

> **Canonical repository source of truth.** This document is the working reference for the Fuel & Free Time brand, website, products, content, community, collaborations, and future opportunities.

*Working first draft for Justin and the project team*

Prepared September 2026

A shared source of truth for the brand, website, products, content, community, collaborations, and future opportunities.

# How to Use This Draft

This draft captures the clearest direction already established for Fuel & Free Time. It is meant to help the project team build with intention while leaving room for Justin to confirm details and change direction. It is not a formal business plan or a promise that every future idea will happen.

**Status key.**

- CONFIRMED means the idea is established enough to guide current work.
- ASPIRATIONAL means it belongs in the future vision but is not yet a commitment.
- TBD means a real decision or verified business fact is still needed.

The strongest conclusion in this draft is that Fuel & Free Time should be built as a lifestyle and media brand with merchandise—not merely as a T-shirt store. The unifying idea is simple: work earns the freedom to spend time on the machines, people, places, and experiences that make life worth living.

# 0 Quick Read

## In My Own Words What Is Fuel and Free Time

**CONFIRMED**

Fuel & Free Time is a lifestyle brand for people who work hard and make their free time count. It connects cars, trucks, motorcycles, boats, fishing, travel, events, garage time, and the people we enjoy those things with. It is about enjoying what you worked for—not waiting forever to live.

## Why I Wanted to Create It

**CONFIRMED**

I wanted a brand that could bring together the things I am actually into without being trapped in one category. A truck guy, motorcycle rider, fisherman, retiree, veteran, weekend traveler, or somebody building something in the garage can all understand the same feeling. We put in the hours so we can own a little more of our time and do something memorable with it.

## What Fuel and Free Time Means to Me

**CONFIRMED**

Fuel is literal—gas, diesel, race fuel, boats, bikes, trucks, aircraft, and anything with an engine. It also means ambition, coffee, family, friendship, and whatever keeps somebody moving. Free time is the part of life that belongs to you: weekends, retirement, a ride after work, a morning on the water, a road trip, a garage night, or time with people who matter.

## Who I Picture Connecting With It

**CONFIRMED**

I picture blue-collar workers, gearheads, riders, boat people, fishermen, veterans, retirees, weekend warriors, builders, travelers, and people who take pride in what they own and what they do. They may have different budgets and hobbies, but they understand that time is valuable and fun is better when it is earned.

## What Somebody Should Feel

**CONFIRMED**

Freedom, pride, motivation, adventure, belonging, nostalgia, humor, and the feeling that the brand is real. It should feel premium enough to be proud of, blue-collar enough to stay grounded, and welcoming enough that it never becomes a private club for people with the most expensive toys.

## What I Hope It Becomes

**ASPIRATIONAL**

A recognizable lifestyle company that combines apparel, content, collaborations, events, and real experiences. The long-term opportunity is a brand people follow even when they are not shopping: stories, builds, road trips, boats, motorcycles, aircraft, interesting people, and events that bring the community together.

# 2 Audience and Belonging

## 2.1 Who This Is For

1.  The worker who spends long weeks on the road or jobsite, then uses the days off to ride, fish, travel, or build something.
2.  The gearhead with a truck, motorcycle, car, boat, UTV, or project that gives them a reason to get out of the house.
3.  The veteran or retiree who finally has more control over time and wants to keep moving, meeting people, and making memories.
4.  The younger rider or builder who is ambitious, works for what they have, and wants a brand that feels authentic rather than corporate.
5.  The couple, family, or group of friends whose best days happen on the road, at the lake, at a show, in the garage, or around a machine.

## 2.2 What Connects Them

**CONFIRMED**

They believe free time is valuable, hobbies are part of identity, and good experiences are worth working for. They appreciate machines but do not need the newest or most expensive one to belong. They respect effort, independence, humor, and people who are genuine.

## 2.3 Boundaries

**CONFIRMED**

- Not overly political or built around culture-war outrage.
- Not fake luxury, rented-success imagery, or status for status alone.
- Not macho just to prove something and not hostile to newcomers.
- Not reckless behavior presented as the brand’s entire personality.
- Not generic racing, fishing, or patriotic merchandise with a logo pasted on.
- Not overly corporate, polished to the point of losing personality, or dependent on trends.
- Not limited to one machine, age group, income level, or hobby.

# 3 Personality Voice and Vibe

## 3.1 If the Brand Were a Person

**CONFIRMED**

Fuel & Free Time would talk straight, joke around, and treat people the same whether they pulled up in a million-dollar car or an old truck they built themselves. It would dress clean but not delicate. On Saturday it might be at a boat launch before sunrise, a car meet by lunch, and in a garage or around a fire that night. It would be confident without needing to tell everybody how important it is.

## 3.2 Strongest Characteristics

Authentic, adventurous, blue-collar, independent, welcoming, funny, premium, and energetic. Supporting traits are nostalgic, American, local, gritty, and motivational.

## 3.3 What It Should Never Feel Like

Cheap, copied, fake, exclusive in the wrong way, overly aggressive, childish, corporate, cluttered, desperate for attention, or disconnected from real people and real experiences.

## 3.4 Language

The voice should be direct, confident, conversational, and occasionally funny. It can sound like something somebody would actually say in a shop, at a boat launch, on a ride, or at an event. Avoid empty hype such as “redefining the lifestyle,” fake motivational speeches, constant profanity, and clichés that could belong to any apparel company.

Working language and recurring ideas: “Gas up. Live free.” “Built to roam.” “Wind therapy.” “Where gravity loses.” “Because working sucks.” “Time is the real horsepower.” The final slogan system should use a small number of repeatable lines rather than putting a different slogan on everything.

## 3.5 Current Messaging

**CONFIRMED**

TIME IS THE REAL HORSEPOWER fits the brand strongly. It shifts attention away from who has the fastest or most expensive machine and toward the one resource everybody values. Horsepower can move a machine, but time is what lets somebody use it, enjoy it, and build a life around it. This should be treated as a lead brand line, with “Gas up. Live free.” retained as a supporting line for product graphics and action-oriented content.

# 4 Visual and Sensory Direction

## 4.1 Look and Feel

**CONFIRMED**

- Sunrise at a boat launch; fog over the water; coolers and rods ready before daylight.
- A clean but used garage at night; weld light, tools, polished metal, grease, and real work.
- A highway or back road at golden hour with a truck, motorcycle, or trailer in motion.
- Machine details: gauges, stitching, paint, chrome, carbon, weathered leather, tires, fuel nozzles, and hands doing the work.
- Air shows, hangars, aircraft panels, the grounded helicopter mobile store, and crowds gathered around something unexpected.
- Louisiana water, fields, cypress, coastal roads, local shops, and authentic Southern settings.
- Friends and families doing something rather than posing like models.

The visual system should balance clean premium presentation with texture and motion. Core neutrals can be washed black, bone, cream, white, charcoal, and workwear tan, with selective bright accents such as lime green, red, aviation blue, burnt orange, or collection-specific colors. Graphics can range from crisp badges to lightly illustrated vehicles, but the subject must remain recognizable and the composition should not become cluttered.

# 5 Products and Commerce

## 5.1 Initial Product Direction

**CONFIRMED**

Launch narrow enough to execute well: premium graphic T-shirts, hats, stickers, and patches. Add hoodies or work shirts only if samples, season, and cash flow support them. The first products should prove the visual identity and fulfillment process before expanding.

## 5.2 Product Philosophy

Mixed, led by premium everyday wear. The main line should be wearable often, with a smaller number of humorous statement pieces and limited collectible drops. Quality of blanks, print, fit, and finishing must be good enough that the product earns repeat wear instead of being novelty merch.

# 6 Content and Storytelling

## 6.1 YouTube and Episodes

**ASPIRATIONAL**

- What Fuels You profiles: real people, what they do, what they have built, and how they spend free time.
- Build and rescue stories: vehicles, boats, motorcycles, equipment, or unusual machines brought back to life.
- Road trips and destination episodes with a purpose, challenge, event, or person at the end.
- Fishing, boating, riding, air-show, garage, and adventure days that entertain without feeling staged.
- Behind the brand: product development, event preparation, mistakes, wins, and the helicopter mobile-store build.
- Collaborations with people whose work and hobbies naturally fit the brand.
- Fuel Run challenges or scavenger-style experiences that connect multiple communities.

Justin can appear on camera as himself rather than trying to become a polished studio host. Friends, owners, builders, customers, and collaborators can carry individual stories. The best mix is entertainment first, real story second, useful information when it naturally belongs, and product promotion kept light enough to preserve trust.

# 8 Website and Digital Experience

## 8.1 First Impression

Within ten seconds, visitors should understand that Fuel & Free Time is a lifestyle brand about working hard, owning your time, and using it on what moves you. They should immediately see strong real-world imagery, a clear lead message, and an obvious route to shop or watch.

After a few minutes, they should know the brand is broader than one vehicle category, recognize the philosophy behind the name, see current products and stories, and understand how to stay connected. Justin should be proud to send the site because it feels distinctive, complete, fast, and real—not like a template filled with stock photos.

**CONFIRMED**
